\section{FITTING METHODOLOGIES AND STABILITY ANALYSIS}

\section{Methode: Fit from $(b_{L}, b_{T})$ space to $(P\cdot b, b^2)$ space}
We do fits in $(b_{L}, b_{T})$ space and then convert them into $(P\cdot b, b^2)$ amplitudes. For example for simple Gaussian fit inn $(b_{L}, b_{T})$ space, we model the real amplitudes as
\begin{align}
    \tAmp^{Re}_{iB}&=\gamma \exp{\left[-\alpha \textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}-\beta \textcolor{red}{b_{T}^{2}}\right]}\\
    &=\gamma \exp{\left[-\alpha \textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}-\beta \left(\textcolor{blue}{b}^{2}-\textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}\right)\right]}\\
    &=\gamma \exp{\left[-\left(\alpha-\beta\right) \textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}-\beta \textcolor{blue}{b}^{2}\right]}\\
   \tAmp^{Re}_{iB} &=\gamma \exp{\left[-\frac{\left(\alpha-\beta\right)}{P_{L}^2} (\textcolor{blue}{P\cdot b})^{2}-\beta \textcolor{blue}{b}^{2}\right]}
\end{align}
and we model the imaginary amplitudes as
\begin{align}
    \tAmp^{Im}_{iB}&=\gamma \textcolor{red}{b_{L}}\exp{\left[-\alpha \textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}-\beta \textcolor{red}{b_{T}^{2}}\right]}\\
    &=\gamma \textcolor{red}{b_{L}}\exp{\left[-\alpha \textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}-\beta \left(\textcolor{blue}{b}^{2}-\textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}\right)\right]}\\
    &=\gamma \textcolor{red}{b_{L}}\exp{\left[-\left(\alpha-\beta\right) \textcolor{red}{b_{L}^{2}}-\beta \textcolor{blue}{b}^{2}\right]}\\
    \tAmp^{Im}_{iB} &=\frac{\gamma}{P_{L}} (\textcolor{blue}{P\cdot b}) \exp{\left[-\frac{\left(\alpha-\beta\right)}{P_{L}^2} (\textcolor{blue}{P\cdot b})^{2}-\beta \textcolor{blue}{b}^{2}\right]}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Finding best Fit: $b\geq3$}
For $P_L=-1$, the range $7\leq\eta\leq10$ is found to be the most stable region for our analysis. We begin the fitting procedure with a simple Gaussian ansatz in $(b_L, b_T)$, which is a common baseline in lattice phenomenology. However, this model yields a high $\chi^2/\text{DoF} \approx 3.54$, indicating that a pure Gaussian decay is too steep to fit the data well.

To address this, we systematically explore several functional variants. We find that introducing a power-law dependence significantly improves the quality of the fit. 

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\linewidth]{Gaussian_type_fit_Real_A2B/FitList_eta78910_p-1.eps}
    \caption{Summary of various functional fits for $\tAmp^{Re}_{2B}$ for $7\leq\eta\leq10$, $b\geq3$ and $P_L=-1$}
    \label{fig:placeholder}
\end{figure}
For $\tAmp^{Re}_{2B}$ for $7\leq\eta\leq10$, $b\geq3$ and $P_L=-1$, the picked model is
{\color{blue}
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tAmp^{Re}_{2B}(b_L, b_T,\eta)&=\frac{ae^{-f\eta}}{(1~+~c\eta^{g}b_{L}^2~+~db_{T}^{2})^{j}}}\\
    \tAmp^{Re}_{2B}(b_L, b_T,\eta)&=\frac{[5.2(12)]e^{-[0.500(15)]\eta}}{(1~+~[0.034(17)]\eta^{[0.50(15)]}b_{L}^2~+~[0.103(29)]b_{T}^{2})^{[2.16(21)]}}
\end{align}
}
