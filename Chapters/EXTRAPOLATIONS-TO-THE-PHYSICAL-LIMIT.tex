\section{EXTRAPOLATIONS TO THE PHYSICAL LIMIT}
\label{sec:physical_limit_extrapolation}

Section~\ref{sec:zetahat_extrapolation_motivation} shows that the amplitudes, the TMD combinations and the Sivers shift change systematically with $1/\hat\zeta$ across the four simulated momenta. Section~\ref{sec:extrap_fit_param_level} extrapolates the fit parameters to $1/\hat\zeta\to0$ using $P_L=-2,-3,-4$, reconstructs the amplitudes and the Sivers shift in this limit, and lists the numerical Fourier-transform integrals over $x$ at $|b_T|=3a$. Section~\ref{sec:factorization_xbt} tests whether the extrapolated Sivers shift and amplitudes factorize in $x$ and $|b_T|$.

\subsection{Motivation for the \texorpdfstring{$\hat{\zeta} \to \infty$}{TEXT} Extrapolation}
\label{sec:zetahat_extrapolation_motivation}

Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methodologies} established stable global simultaneous fits to the invariant amplitudes $\tAmp_{2B}$ and $\tAmp_{12B}$ at each of our four simulated nucleon momenta, corresponding to the finite Collins-Soper parameters $\hat\zeta = 0.090772,\ 0.294367,\ 0.539684,\ 0.785756$ for $P_L=-1,-2,-3,-4$ respectively (Table~\ref{tab:zeta_directions}). As discussed in Section~\ref{sec:collins_soper_parameter}, these finite-$\hat\zeta$ results follow from the space-like staple direction that our Euclidean lattice geometry requires; the physical light-cone TMDs are recovered only in the limit $\hat\zeta \to \infty$. To compare our lattice results with phenomenological extractions of the Sivers function, we must therefore extrapolate each finite-momentum result to this physical limit.

Figure~\ref{fig:zetahat_discrete_slices} shows why we need this extrapolation. At fixed transverse quark separation $|b_T| = 3a \approx 0.34$ fm, we evaluate the reconstructed amplitudes $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}}$ and $i\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}$, the corresponding unpolarized and Sivers TMD combinations $\tilde f_1^{(0)} = 2\tAmp_{2B}$ and $\tilde f_{1T}^{\perp(1)} = -2\tAmp_{12B}$, and the resulting Sivers shift $\langle k_y \rangle_{TU}$ and $x\langle k_y \rangle_{TU}$, as continuous functions of $x$ at each of our four simulated momenta. Plotted against $1/\hat\zeta$, all six quantities vary systematically with the momentum. None of our finite lattice momenta has reached the physical limit, so the results can be interpreted physically only after the extrapolation to $1/\hat\zeta \to 0$ carried out in the rest of this chapter.

\subsection{Extrapolation at fit-parameters level}
\label{sec:extrap_fit_param_level}

The lowest simulated momentum, $P_L=-1$ ($\hat\zeta = 0.090772$), sits too close to the origin in $1/\hat\zeta$ to resolve the curvature required to anchor an extrapolation; including it risks biasing the fit away from the true asymptotic ($\hat\zeta \to \infty$) behavior rather than constraining it. We therefore exclude $P_L=-1$ and extrapolate using only the three higher, better-separated momenta $P_L = -2,-3,-4$.

Rather than extrapolate the amplitudes point by point in $x$, we extrapolate each of the twelve parameters of the global simultaneous-fit models directly: the four parameters $\{a,c,d,j\}$ appearing in each of the coupled Lorentzian models for $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}}$, $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}$, and $\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Re}}$ (Eq.~(\ref{eq:model_ReA2B})-(\ref{eq:model_ImA2B})).
% TODO(Hari): the global fit also has f, k1, k2 (Fig. cov_matrix_2d_P1). State how they enter the extrapolation (f cancels in the Sivers shift, but what about the amplitude plots, and how is c(eta) with k1, k2 evaluated?).
In these models $c$ multiplies $b_L^2 = (P\cdot b)^2/P_L^2$ and $a_{\text{im}}$ multiplies $b_L = (P\cdot b)/(-P_L)$, so the parameters $c_{\text{re}}$, $c_{\text{im}}$, and $c_{\text{reA12B}}$ are first rescaled by $1/P_L^2$ and $a_{\text{im}}$ by $1/P_L$. After rescaling they multiply powers of the invariant $P\cdot b$ directly (up to an overall sign for $a_{\text{im}}$), so all three momenta can be fit on a common footing. For the resulting $\hat\zeta$-dependence of each rescaled parameter, we tested two functional forms,
\begin{equation}
    p(\hat\zeta) = c + \frac{d}{\hat\zeta} \qquad \text{and} \qquad p(\hat\zeta) = c + \frac{d}{\hat\zeta^2} \ ,
    \label{eq:zetahat_extrap_ansatz}
\end{equation}
using correlated Jackknife fits to the three data points. The quadratic ansatz consistently gave a lower $\chi^2/\text{dof}$ than the linear form across the twelve parameters, and we adopt it for every extrapolation shown below. The extrapolated, physical-limit value of each parameter is its fitted intercept $c$ at $1/\hat\zeta=0$.

\subsection{Testing Factorization in \texorpdfstring{$x$}{TEXT} and \texorpdfstring{$|b_T|$}{TEXT} at the Physical Limit}
\label{sec:factorization_xbt}

Section~\ref{sec:factorization_test} tested factorization of the position-space amplitudes in $(P\cdot b)$ and $b^2$. Here we ask a different question: whether the extrapolated distributions factorize in the momentum fraction $x$ and the transverse separation $|b_T|$.


Figures~\ref{fig:extrapolated_amplitude_3d} and~\ref{fig:momenta_vs_extrapolated} show that the extrapolated Sivers shift and amplitudes depend on both $x$ and $|b_T|$ jointly. We ask whether this joint dependence is separable, that is, whether the extrapolated Sivers shift factorizes as
\begin{equation}
    \langle k_y \rangle_{TU}(x,|b_T|) \overset{?}{=} F(x)\,G(|b_T|) \ ,
\end{equation}
with all of the $x$-shape carried by $F(x)$ and all of the $|b_T|$-shape by $G(|b_T|)$. Such a factorization is not automatic in our fit ansatz. Each amplitude enters only through the combination $d_X(b^2) = 1 + d_X\,b^2$ inside a Bessel function that also carries the $x$-dependence,
\begin{equation}
    \tAmp_X(x,b^2) \ \propto\ N_X(b^2)\ |x|^{-1/2+j_X}\ K_{\nu_X}\!\left(|x|\sqrt{d_X(b^2)/c_X}\right) \ ,
\end{equation}
% TODO(Hari): this closed form (and nu_X, N_X) is not derived earlier in the thesis. Add the Fourier transform of the power-law model in the Fitting chapter (Simultaneous Fitting Strategy) or an appendix, and cite it here.
so $|b_T|$ rescales the argument of the same Bessel function that carries the $x$-dependence, rather than multiplying it by an overall $x$-independent prefactor. The Sivers shift is a ratio of three such amplitudes ($\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}}$, $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}$, $\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Re}}$), each with its own independently fitted $(j_X,d_X)$; an exact factorization would require these parameters to coincide across the three amplitudes so that the $x$- and $|b_T|$-dependence separates. Whether the extrapolated fit comes close enough to that coincidence is a numerical question, which we answer below.

Section~\ref{sec:factorization_metric} defines the relative distance from a factorized form, and Sections~\ref{sec:factorization_sivers_test} and~\ref{sec:factorization_amplitude_test} apply it to the Sivers shift and to the individual amplitudes.

\subsubsection{Quantifying the Distance from a Factorized Form}
\label{sec:factorization_metric}

Given a function of two variables $f(x,|b_T|)$, we quantify how far it is from a factorized form $g(x)h(|b_T|)$ by finding the closest such pair in the least-squares sense and reporting the residual. Explicitly, we minimize
\begin{equation}
    J = \int dx\, d|b_T| \, \big(f(x,|b_T|) - g(x)h(|b_T|)\big)^2
\end{equation}
over all functions $g$ and $h$. Setting the functional derivatives with respect to $g(x)$ and $h(|b_T|)$ to zero gives
\begin{align}
    0 &= \frac{\delta J}{\delta g(x)} = \int d|b_T|\, f(x,|b_T|)\,h(|b_T|) - g(x)\int d|b_T|\, h^2(|b_T|) \ , \\
    0 &= \frac{\delta J}{\delta h(|b_T|)} = \int dx\, f(x,|b_T|)\,g(x) - h(|b_T|)\int dx\, g^2(x) \ ,
\end{align}
which can be solved for $h$ in terms of $g$,
\begin{equation}
    h(|b_T|) = \frac{\int dx\, f(x,|b_T|)\,g(x)}{\int dx\, g^2(x)} \ ,
    \label{eq:factorization_h_solution}
\end{equation}
and reinserted to eliminate $h$, giving a closed, self-consistent equation for $g$ alone:
\begin{equation}
    g(x) = \left[\int d|b_T| \left[\frac{\int dx'\, f(x',|b_T|)\,g(x')}{\int dx'\, g^2(x')}\right]^2\right]^{-1} \int d|b_T|\, f(x,|b_T|)\,\frac{\int dx'\, f(x',|b_T|)\,g(x')}{\int dx'\, g^2(x')} \ .
    \label{eq:factorization_g_selfconsistent}
\end{equation}
We solve Eq.~\eqref{eq:factorization_g_selfconsistent} iteratively. Starting from an initial guess $g=1$, we evaluate the right-hand side to obtain a refined $g$, reinsert it, and repeat until $g$ (and the resulting relative distance below) stops changing within a fixed tolerance. For the grids used here, convergence is rapid in practice.

We then compare the residual $\sqrt{J}$ of the optimal separable pair $g(x)h(|b_T|)$ with the overall size of $f$, $N = \int dx\, d|b_T|\, f^2(x,|b_T|)$, which gives the relative distance from a factorized form:
\begin{equation}
    \text{relative distance} = \sqrt{J/N} \ .
    \label{eq:factorization_relative_distance}
\end{equation}
A relative distance of zero indicates exact factorization; the closer to zero, the better $F(x)G(|b_T|)$ approximates the true joint $(x,|b_T|)$ dependence. In practice, we replace the continuum integrals with sums over a discrete $(x,|b_T|)$ grid, apply the iteration of Eq.~\eqref{eq:factorization_g_selfconsistent} independently to each Jackknife sample of the extrapolated amplitudes, and reduce the resulting distribution of relative distances to a Jackknife mean and error. We repeat the full test in four $x$-windows, $[0,1]$, $[0.1,0.9]$, $[0.2,0.8]$, and $[0.3,0.8]$, to check that the conclusion is not an artifact of the amplitudes' behavior near $x=0$.

\subsubsection{Factorization Test for the Sivers Shift}
\label{sec:factorization_sivers_test}

The extrapolated fit parameters already suggest, before any distance test, that exact factorization is unlikely. As noted in Section~\ref{sec:factorization_xbt}, factorization requires the Bessel-function order $j_X$ and width parameter $d_X$ to coincide across the three input amplitudes; the extrapolated values are
\begin{equation}
    j_{\text{re}} = 2.0667(69), \quad j_{\text{im}} = 0.9921(92), \quad j_{\text{reA12B}} = 2.0026(40) \ ,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    d_{\text{re}} = 0.139(12), \quad d_{\text{im}} = 0.183(14), \quad d_{\text{reA12B}} = 0.105(18) \ .
\end{equation}
The two $j$ parameters entering the real amplitudes, $j_{\text{re}}$ and $j_{\text{reA12B}}$, agree with each other, but $j_{\text{im}} \approx 1$ differs clearly from both. The three $d$ parameters lie closer together but are still resolved from one another well outside their Jackknife uncertainties. This mismatch in the parameters, especially in $j$, gives a separate reason to expect a nonzero residual from exact factorization.

Ratios of the Sivers shift at fixed $x$ or fixed $|b_T|$ show the same non-factorization more directly. If $\langle k_y \rangle_{TU}(x,|b_T|) = F(x)G(|b_T|)$ exactly, then $R_b(x) \equiv \langle k_y \rangle_{TU}(x,|b_T|)/\langle k_y \rangle_{TU}(x,|b_T|_{\text{ref}}) = G(|b_T|)/G(|b_T|_{\text{ref}})$ must be flat in $x$ for every choice of $|b_T|$ and reference $|b_T|_{\text{ref}}$, and likewise $R_x(|b_T|) \equiv \langle k_y \rangle_{TU}(x,|b_T|)/\langle k_y \rangle_{TU}(x_{\text{ref}},|b_T|) = F(x)/F(x_{\text{ref}})$ must be flat in $|b_T|$ for every $x$ and $x_{\text{ref}}$. Requiring this for every pair of references is both necessary and sufficient for factorization, so the ratio test is a complete test rather than a heuristic proxy. Figures~\ref{fig:factorization_ratio_x_sivers} and~\ref{fig:factorization_ratio_bT_sivers} show that neither ratio is flat.

\subsubsection{Factorization Test at the Amplitude Level}
\label{sec:factorization_amplitude_test}

The Sivers-shift test above probes factorization of the full ratio $\langle k_y \rangle_{TU} = -m_N \tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Re}}/(\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}} - i\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}})$, in which some of the non-factorized behavior of the individual amplitudes could in principle cancel. We therefore repeat the same distance test of Section~\ref{sec:factorization_metric} on each extrapolated amplitude separately: $\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Re}}$, $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}}$, $i\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}$, and the combination $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}} - i\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}$ (proportional to $\tilde f_1^{[1](0)}$) that appears in the Sivers-shift denominator. Table~\ref{tab:factorization_amplitudes} lists the resulting relative distances, using the same $|b_T|\in[0.3421,0.9403]$ fm grid and four $x$-windows as the Sivers-shift test.

Every amplitude has a nonzero relative distance from factorized form in every window. For the widest window, $x\in[0,1]$, the amplitude-level residuals ($8$-$19\%$) exceed the corresponding Sivers-ratio residual ($5.7\%$, Table~\ref{tab:factorization_sivers}), so the ratio structure of the Sivers shift partially, but not completely, cancels the non-factorized behavior of the individual amplitudes. All four amplitude-level residuals shrink as the $x$-window narrows away from $x=0$. This is consistent with the Bessel-function singularity structure of each amplitude near the origin, which differs between amplitudes because of the parameter mismatch identified in Section~\ref{sec:factorization_sivers_test}.

The Sivers-shift and amplitude-level tests agree. Neither the individual amplitudes nor the Sivers-shift ratio built from them is exactly factorized in $x$ and $|b_T|$, but the deviation from a factorized form is a modest, well-resolved residual and not a qualitative failure. The Sivers shift itself is factorized to within roughly $6$-$8\%$ in the relative $L_2$ sense. This is smaller than the $8$-$19\%$ residuals of the amplitudes that enter it, which points to a partial cancellation of non-factorized behavior in the ratio. The residual traces back directly to the extrapolated fit parameters: the Bessel-function order $j_{\text{im}}\approx1$ governing $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}$ differs from $j_{\text{re}},j_{\text{reA12B}}\approx2$ governing the two real amplitudes, and the three width parameters $d_X$ are likewise resolved from one another well outside their Jackknife uncertainties. We conclude that an exactly factorized ansatz $F(x)G(|b_T|)$ would be a good, but not exact, approximation to the extracted Sivers shift.
