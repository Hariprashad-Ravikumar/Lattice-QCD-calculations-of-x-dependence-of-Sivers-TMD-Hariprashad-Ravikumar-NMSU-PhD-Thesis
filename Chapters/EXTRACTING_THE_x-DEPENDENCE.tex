\section{EXTRACTING THE \texorpdfstring{$x-$}{TEXT}DEPENDENCE}
\label{sec:x_dependence}

\subsection{The Sivers Shift as a Function of Momentum Fraction}
In order to connect our lattice observables to the physical, $x$-dependent Transverse Momentum Dependent Parton Distribution Functions (TMDs), we must isolate the relevant amplitudes from the correlation function. Focusing on the leading-twist unpolarized quark distribution, the correlator is parameterized by the Lorentz-invariant amplitudes $\tAmp_{2B}$ and $\tAmp_{12B}$:
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{2} \bra{P,S}\ \bar{q}(0)\ \gamma^{+}\ \WlineC{\mathcal{C}_\bvec}\ q(\bvec)\ \ket{P,S}=2P^{+}\left(\tAmp_{2B} 
		+ i \mN \epsilon_{ij} \vect{\bvec}_i \vect{S}_j\, \tAmp_{12B} \right) \ .
\end{align}
Our primary goal is to extract the generalized Sivers shift, $\langle \vect{k}_y \rangle_{TU}$, as a continuous function of the longitudinal momentum fraction $x = k^+ / P^+$. The Sivers shift is defined phenomenologically as the ratio of the first transverse momentum moment of the Sivers function to the unpolarized distribution. 

Because $x$ and the longitudinal spatial separation $b \cdot P$ act as Fourier conjugate variables, mapping the $x$-dependence fundamentally requires performing a full Fourier transform of these amplitudes with respect to $b \cdot P$. Analytically, the $x$-dependent Sivers shift takes the form:
\begin{align}
   \langle \vect{k}_y \rangle_{TU}(\vprp{\bvec}^2,x,\zetahat,\eta v \tcdot P) 
	&\ \equiv\ \mN \frac{\tilde f_{1T}^{\perp(1)}(\vprp{\bvec}^2;\zetahat,\ldots,\eta v \tcdot P)}{\tilde f_1^{(0)}(\vprp{\bvec}^2;\zetahat,\ldots,\eta v \tcdot P)} \nonumber \\
 &=-m_{N} \frac{\int d(b\cdot P)e^{ix(b\cdot P)} \tAmp_{12B}(\bvec^2,\bvec \tcdot P,(\bvec \tcdot P) R(\zetahat^2)/\mN^2,-1/(\mN\zetahat)^2,\eta v \tcdot P)}{\int d(b\cdot P)e^{ix(b\cdot P)}\tAmp_{2B}(\bvec^2,\bvec \tcdot P,(\bvec \tcdot P) R(\zetahat^2)/\mN^2,-1/(\mN\zetahat)^2,\eta v \tcdot P)} \nonumber \\
 &=-m_{N} \frac{\int d(b\cdot P)e^{ix(b\cdot P)} \left[\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Re}}(\cdots)+i\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Im}}(\cdots)\right]}{\int d(b\cdot P)e^{ix(b\cdot P)}\left[\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}}(\cdots)+i\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}(\cdots)\right]}\\
 &=-m_{N} \frac{\int d(b\cdot P) \left[\cos{\left(x(b\cdot P)\right)}\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Re}}(\cdots)-\sin{\left(x(b\cdot P)\right)}\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Im}}(\cdots)\right]}{\int d(b\cdot P)\left[\cos{\left(x(b\cdot P)\right)}\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}}(\cdots)-\sin{\left(x(b\cdot P)\right)}\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}(\cdots)\right]} \ .
 \end{align}
 Expanding the Fourier phase factor into its trigonometric components isolates the amplitudes' real and imaginary contributions to the Sivers shift. Because the integration over $d(b \cdot P)$ is performed over a symmetric domain, the non-vanishing terms of the integral are completely dictated by the parity of the integrands with respect to $b \cdot P$. The trigonometric weights $\cos\left(x(b\cdot P)\right)$ and $\sin\left(x(b\cdot P)\right)$ are inherently even and odd, respectively.

This harmonic symmetry elegantly filters the invariant amplitudes based on their behavior under Hermitian conjugation. As constrained by fundamental symmetries, the real components $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Re}}$ and $\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Re}}$ must be even functions of $b \cdot P$, while the imaginary components $\tAmp_{2B}^{\text{Im}}$ and $\tAmp_{12B}^{\text{Im}}$ must be odd. Consequently, only the symmetric products, i.e., the even real amplitudes coupled with the cosine term, and the odd imaginary amplitudes coupled with the sine term, survive the integration. The operational challenge now lies in extracting these specific parity components from the discrete lattice data. The following section describes how we strategically construct our correlation functions to extract these distinct real and imaginary amplitudes.


\subsection{The Collins-Soper Parameter (\texorpdfstring{$\hat{\zeta}$}{TEXT})}
A fundamental complication in calculating TMDs is the presence of rapidity divergences induced by the gauge link extending to infinity. In the continuum, these divergences are regulated by tilting the Wilson line slightly off the light cone. In our Euclidean lattice framework, the staple direction $v$ must be strictly space-like ($v^2 < 0$). The Lorentz-invariant quantity that characterizes the proximity of our space-like gauge link to the physical light-like trajectory is the Collins-Soper parameter:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\zeta} = \frac{v \cdot P}{m_N \sqrt{|v^2|}} \ .
\end{equation}
The physical TMD limit is recovered by extrapolating $\hat{\zeta} \to \infty$. In practice, since $v$ is a purely spatial vector on the lattice ($v_0 = 0$), increasing $\hat{\zeta}$ requires simulating the nucleon at progressively higher spatial momenta $\vect{P}$. Tracking the evolution of the Sivers shift across multiple values of $\hat{\zeta}$ is therefore an indispensable step for phenomenological predictions.

\subsection{Kinematic Constraints for \texorpdfstring{$b\cdot P \neq 0$}{TEXT}}
To perform the Fourier transform over $b \cdot P$, we require a lattice geometry where $b \cdot P$ is non-zero. However, the Lorentz-invariant arguments of the amplitudes are not independent. Our chosen frame ($b^+=0, \vprp{P}=0$) enforces the constraint:
\begin{align}
    \frac{v \tcdot b}{v \tcdot P} = b \tcdot P \frac{R(\zetahat^2)}{\mN^2} \ ,
\end{align}
where $R(\zetahat^2) = 1 - \sqrt{1 + \zetahat^{-2}}$. Expanding the dot products in a frame where the momentum is directed along the 1-axis ($P = (E, P_1, 0, 0)$), we find:
\begin{align}
    b_{1}|v|\left[\frac{P_{1}}{m_{N}}\left(\hat{\zeta}-\sqrt{\hat{\zeta}^2+1}\right)+\hat{\zeta}\frac{m_{N}}{P_{1}}\right]=\vec{v}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}_{T} \ .
\end{align}
To satisfy this equation over a continuous range of $b \cdot P$ without coupling the transverse separation $\vec{b}_T$ directly to the longitudinal staple geometry, we deliberately orient the transverse vectors orthogonally. By choosing $\vec{v}_{T}=(0,v_{3})$ and $\vec{b}_{T}=(b_{2},0)$, the right-hand side identically vanishes ($\vec{v}_{T}\cdot\vec{b}_{T} = 0$). 

\subsubsection{Explicit Calculation for \texorpdfstring{$P_1=-1$}{TEXT}}
Setting the right-hand side to zero implies that the bracketed term must vanish. This uniquely fixes the required Collins-Soper parameter for a given lattice momentum. Using the lattice ensemble parameters, the nucleon mass and the lowest momentum mode are:
\begin{align}
    \mN &= \frac{0.6228(28)}{0.11403\text{ fm}} =  \frac{0.6228(28)\times 197.32698 \text{ MeV}}{0.11403} = 1077.8(48)\text{ MeV} \ , \\
    P_{1}&=-\frac{2\pi}{L}=-\frac{2\pi}{32\times 0.11403\text{ fm}}=-339.3\text{ MeV} \ .
\end{align}
Substituting these values into the constraint equation yields:
\begin{align}
   \Longrightarrow \left[\frac{P_{1}}{m_{N}}\left(\hat{\zeta}-\sqrt{\hat{\zeta}^2+1}\right)+\hat{\zeta}\frac{m_{N}}{P_{1}}\right]&=0 \nonumber \\
    \Longrightarrow \hat{\zeta} &=\sqrt{\frac{P_{1}^2}{m_{N}^{2}\left(\frac{m_{N}^{2}}{P_{1}^{2}}+2\right)}} \nonumber \\
    \hat{\zeta} &= \pm 0.09077(75) \nonumber \\
    \Aboxed{\hat{\zeta} &= \pm 0.090772} \ .
\end{align}
With $\hat{\zeta}$ uniquely determined, we can derive the specific orientation of the space-like staple vector $v$. From the definition of $\hat{\zeta}$:
\begin{align}
    \hat{\zeta}&=\frac{-v_{1}P_{1}}{|v|\mN}=\frac{-v_{1}P_{1}}{\left(\sqrt{v_{1}^{2}+0^{2}+v_{3}^{2}}\right)m_{N}} \ .
\end{align}
Solving for the longitudinal staple component $v_1$ in terms of the transverse component $v_3$:
\begin{align}
    \Longrightarrow\Aboxed{v_{1}=\pm 0.301~v_{3}} \ .
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Off-Axis \texorpdfstring{$\vect{v}$}{TEXT} and Spatial Interpolation}

To evaluate the correlation function as a function of the staple extent, we scale the direction vector $\vect{v}$ by an increment $\eta$, and $\vect{v}$ is a unit vector. The physical length of the staple is thus governed by the coordinate $\eta \vect{v}/a$. Because $\vect{v}$ points in a continuous, off-axis direction, scaling it by integer values of $\eta$ generally yields target coordinates that fall somewhere between the discrete Cartesian lattice sites. 

 To access the continuous off-axis coordinate $\eta \vect{v}/a$, we employ a spatial bounding and interpolation strategy. Rather than constructing a single, potentially biased step-wise path (a "staircase") that roughly approximates the vector, we explicitly compute the observable at multiple distinct integer lattice sites surrounding the exact ideal target coordinate. 
 
 These bounding coordinates are reached via specific permutations of fundamental links. Within our methodology, a path sequence denoted as $\{1,3,1\}$ corresponds to a single link in the $\hat{x_{1}}$ direction, followed by one in the $\hat{x_{3}}$ direction, and a final link in the $\hat{x_{1}}$ direction, successfully landing at the physical integer coordinate $(2,0,1)$. By systematically varying these step sequences, we gather data at three to four physically adjacent lattice sites (e.g., on either side of the target along the $x^3$ slice). 

Once these bounding matrix elements are computed, we perform a spatial interpolation inside the Jackknife resampling loop to extract the value precisely at the continuous target coordinate $\eta \vect{v}/a$. This interpolation drastically mitigates discretization artifacts and staircase errors associated with off-axis Wilson lines. A detailed mathematical description of this interpolation procedure is deferred to the subsequent section. The ideal continuous trajectories, alongside their corresponding discrete bounding paths and link sequences for each momentum transfer, are detailed in Figures \ref{fig:v_path_P-1} through \ref{fig:eta_table_P-4}.

\subsubsection{Spatial Interpolation of Off-Axis Coordinates}
\label{sec:spatial_interpolation}

As established in the previous section, the ideal spatial trajectory of the gauge link, $\eta \vect{v}/a$, rarely falls on an exact integer lattice site. To evaluate the correlation function along this continuous trajectory, we compute the non-local matrix elements at several discrete bounding coordinates and interpolate between them. 

Before interpolating, we must account for path degeneracy. Because the lattice gauge links are restricted to the Cartesian axes, there are multiple step-wise permutations (different side-link paths) that terminate at the exact same physical lattice coordinate. While these paths are geometrically equivalent in terms of their endpoints, they traverse different local gauge field configurations and thus acquire distinct discretization artifacts and gauge noise. As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:example_2path}, we average the correlation functions evaluated over all degenerate paths that share the same endpoint. This path-averaging procedure significantly improves the signal-to-noise ratio before any interpolation is performed.

\begin{figure}[htpb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.25\linewidth]{plots/kinematics_interpolation/Example_v_plot_with_2path.pdf}
    \caption{Illustration of path degeneracy. Two distinct step-wise gauge link permutations (e.g., solid and dashed lines) terminate at the identical discrete lattice coordinate. The correlators from these equivalent paths are averaged to suppress local gauge noise prior to interpolation.}
    \label{fig:example_2path}
\end{figure}

Once the path-averaged correlators are obtained at the discrete bounding sites, we perform a 1-dimensional interpolation. For a fixed transverse staple extent $v_3$, the kinematic constraint dictates an ideal continuous longitudinal component, $v_1^{\text{constraint}}$. The surrounding discrete lattice sites provide correlators at actual simulated coordinates, $v_1^{\text{lattice}}$. We define the fractional deviation from the ideal path as:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta v_1 = \frac{v_1^{\text{constraint}} - v_1^{\text{lattice}}}{a} \ .
\end{equation}
By calculating the observable at three or more bounding lattice sites, we generate a set of data points as a function of this deviation $\Delta v_1$. Within each Jackknife resampling bin (to rigorously preserve the covariance and statistical error), we perform a quadratic interpolation over these points. The physical amplitude lying precisely on the continuous constraint trajectory is then extracted by evaluating the interpolating polynomial exactly at $\Delta v_1 = 0$. 

The core Mathematica logic executed inside the data analysis pipeline to perform this quadratic interpolation is shown in the snippet below:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Mathematica, basicstyle=\ttfamily\footnotesize, frame=single, numbers=left, breaklines=true, breakatwhitespace=true, caption={Core interpolation logic for evaluating the constrained correlator.}, label={lst:interpolation}]
(* Pair fractional deviation (x) with path-averaged data (y) *)
plotforinterpolation = Transpose[{v1dataforInterpolation, 
                                  dataforInterpolation}];

(* Construct quadratic interpolation over bounding points *)
fninterpol = Interpolation[plotforinterpolation, 
                           InterpolationOrder -> 2];

(* Evaluate exactly on the constraint trajectory (deviation = 0) *)
interpol0 = {{0, fninterpol[0]}};
\end{lstlisting}

A representative example of this interpolation procedure is shown in Figure \ref{fig:interpolation_ex} for a specific staple extent ($\eta=7$) and momentum transfer ($P_1 = -1$). The red data points represent the path-averaged lattice evaluations at the discrete bounding sites, plotted against their deviation from the ideal trajectory. The solid gray curve denotes the quadratic interpolation, and the blue point highlights the final extracted physical value at $\Delta v_1 = 0$. This procedure is systematically applied across all Dirac structures ($\Gamma$) and separately for both the real ($b\cdot P$-even) and imaginary ($b\cdot P$-odd) parts of the amplitudes.

\subsection{Quark Separation Vectors (\texorpdfstring{$\vect{b}$}{TEXT}) and Amplitude Parity}
\label{sec:b_separation_paths}

In addition to the staple vector $\vect{v}$, the correlation function depends fundamentally on the spatial separation between the initial and final quark fields, $\vect{b}$. As established, the real and imaginary parts of the invariant amplitudes ($\tAmp_{iB}^{\text{Re}}$ and $\tAmp_{iB}^{\text{Im}}$) are strictly even and odd functions of the longitudinal separation variable $b \cdot P$, respectively. 

Because the nucleon momentum in our lattice setup is fixed along the $\hat{x_{1}}$-axis ($P = P_1 \hat{x_{1}}$), the longitudinal separation is directly proportional to the $\hat{x_{1}}$-component of the separation vector: $b \cdot P = -P_1 b_1$. Therefore, to isolate the real and imaginary parts of the amplitudes, we must evaluate the correlator at both positive and negative values of $b_1$. By taking the symmetric (even) and antisymmetric (odd) linear combinations of the correlators evaluated at $+b_1$ and $-b_1$, we can explicitly project out the real and imaginary amplitudes required for the $x$-dependent Fourier transform.

To perform a robust numerical Fourier transform over $b \cdot P$, it is imperative to sample a wide and dense grid of the separation vector $\vect{b}$. Figure \ref{fig:b_phase_space} illustrates the parameter space targeted in our calculations. \subsubsection{Path Averaging for \texorpdfstring{$\vect{b}$}{TEXT}}

Similar to the logic applied to the off-axis staple vector $\vect{v}$, the off-axis separation vector $\vect{b}$ suffers from path degeneracy. Multiple distinct sequences of orthogonal lattice links can terminate at the identical spatial coordinate $\vect{b}$. Because these different paths traverse slightly different local gauge field fluctuations, computing the observable along just one path introduces arbitrary discretization noise. 

To mitigate this, we average the correlation function over multiple equivalent step-wise paths that share the same origin and endpoint, as illustrated for a single coordinate in Figure \ref{fig:b_path_example}. Applying this path-averaging strategy across the entire target grid yields a comprehensive network of smoothed off-axis trajectories, visualized in Figure \ref{fig:b_full_network}. This path-averaged dataset provides the statistically robust, parity-separated amplitudes required for the final Fourier transform.

\begin{figure}[htpb]

\subsection{Solving for Real and Imaginary Amplitudes \texorpdfstring{$\tAmp_{iB}^{\text{Re}}$ and $\tAmp_{iB}^{\text{Im}}$}{TEXT}}
\label{sec:solving_amplitudes}

To extract the physical Sivers shift from the lattice, we must isolate the specific invariant amplitudes $\tAmp_{2B}$ and $\tAmp_{12B}$ from the raw correlator data. Let us start with the standard parametrization of the unsubtracted correlator \cite{Musch:2011er}:
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^\mu]}_{\unsub} & = 
		P^\mu\, \tAmp_2 - i \mN^2 \bvec^\mu\, \tAmp_3
		- i \mN \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu \bvec_\alpha S_\beta\, \tAmp_{12} \nonumber \\ &
		~~+ \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^\mu\, \tBmp_1 
		+ \frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu v_\alpha S_\beta\, \tBmp_7  
		- \frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} \bvec_\nu v_\alpha S_\beta\, \tBmp_8 \nonumber \\ &
		~~- \frac{\mN^3}{v \tcdot P} (\bvec \tcdot S) \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu \bvec_\alpha v_\beta\, \tBmp_9
		- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu \bvec_\alpha v_\beta \tBmp_{10}
\end{align}
While this parametrization describes a continuous gauge link extending to infinity, for practical lattice calculations, it is highly advantageous to adopt an equivalent representation where the structures explicitly depend on the finite staple extent $\eta$. This ensures the parametrization remains mathematically well-defined even in the $v \cdot P = 0$ limit. This alternative form is obtained by making the substitution $v \rightarrow \eta v$ and extracting the scaling factors $(v \tcdot P)^{-n}$:
 \begin{align}
    \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^\mu]}_{\unsub}=\frac{1}{2} R_{\gamma^{\mu}} E_N & =  
		P^\mu\, \tilde a_2 - i \mN^2 \bvec^\mu\, \tilde a_3
		- i \mN \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu \bvec_\alpha S_\beta\, \tilde a_{12} \nonumber \\ &
		~~+ \mN^2 \eta v^\mu\, \tilde b_1 
		+ \mN \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu \eta v_\alpha S_\beta\, \tilde b_7  
		-  i \mN^3 \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} \bvec_\nu \eta v_\alpha S_\beta\, \tilde b_8 \nonumber \\ &
		~~- \mN^3 (\bvec \tcdot S) \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu \bvec_\alpha \eta v_\beta\, \tilde b_9
		-i \mN^3 (v \tcdot S) \epsilon^{\mu \nu \alpha \beta} P_\nu \bvec_\alpha \eta^2 v_\beta \tilde b_{10}
\end{align}
To evaluate this tensor equation, we project it onto the specific kinematics optimized for our lattice setup. We choose a frame where the nucleon moves along the negative $\hat{1}$-axis, the spin is polarized along the $\hat{3}$-axis, and the separation and staple vectors lie in the transverse/longitudinal planes. Explicitly, we set $P^{\mu}=(E_N, -|\vec{P}^1|,0,0)$, $b^{\mu}=(0,b_{1},b_{2},0)$, $v^{\mu}=\pm n^{\prime}(0,v_{1},0,v_{3})$, and $S^{\mu}=(0,0,0,1)$. Expanding the sum over Lorentz indices for the temporal component ($\mu = 0$) and explicitly cancelling the vanishing terms (such as $b^0 = 0$, $v^0 = 0$, and $\bvec \tcdot S = 0$), we find:
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{0}]}_{\unsub} & = 
		P^0\, \tAmp_2 - \cancelto{0}{i \mN^2 \bvec^0\, \tAmp_3}
		+ i \mN \underbrace{\epsilon^{0 123}}_{1} |\vec{P}^1| \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12} \nonumber \\ &
		~~+ \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^0\, \tBmp_1 }
		+ \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{0 113} P_1 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_7  }+ \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{0 133} P_1 v_3 S_3\, \tBmp_7  }
		 \nonumber \\ &
		~~- \frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \underbrace{\epsilon^{0 213}}_{-1} \bvec_2 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_8 - \cancelto{0}{\frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P}\epsilon^{0 113} \bvec_1 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_8}- \cancelto{0}{\frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P}\epsilon^{0 133} \bvec_1 v_3 S_3\, \tBmp_8}- \cancelto{0}{\frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P}\epsilon^{0 233} \bvec_2 v_3 S_3\, \tBmp_8}\nonumber \\ &
		~~+ \frac{\mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \cancelto{0}{(\bvec \tcdot S)} \underbrace{\epsilon^{0 123}}_{1} |\vec{P}_1| \bvec_2 v_3\, \tBmp_9- \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN^3}{v \tcdot P} (\bvec \tcdot S) \epsilon^{0 121} P_1 \bvec_2 v_1\, \tBmp_9}- \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN^3}{v \tcdot P} (\bvec \tcdot S) \epsilon^{0 113} P_1 \bvec_1 v_3\, \tBmp_9} \nonumber \\ &
        - \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN^3}{v \tcdot P} (\bvec \tcdot S) \epsilon^{0 111} P_1 \bvec_1 v_1\, \tBmp_9} + \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \underbrace{\epsilon^{0 123}}_{1} |\vec{P}_1| \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10}
		- \cancelto{0}{\frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \epsilon^{0 121} P_1 \bvec_2 v_1 \tBmp_{10}} \nonumber \\ &
        - \cancelto{0}{\frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \epsilon^{0 111} P_1 \bvec_1 v_1 \tBmp_{10}} - \cancelto{0}{\frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \epsilon^{0 113} P_1 \bvec_1 v_3 \tBmp_{10}}\\
  \Aboxed{ \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{0}]}_{\unsub}& = P^0\, \tAmp_2  + i \mN  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P}  \bvec_2 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_8+ \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  |P_1| \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10}}
\end{align}
Repeating this tensor contraction for the longitudinal spatial component ($\mu = 1$), taking care to track the signs of the Levi-Civita symbol, yields:
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{1}]}_{\unsub} & = 
		-|\vec{P}^1|\, \tAmp_2 - i \mN^2 \bvec^1\, \tAmp_3
		- i \mN \underbrace{\epsilon^{1 023}}_{-1} P_0 \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12} \nonumber \\ &
		~~+ \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^1\, \tBmp_1 
		+ \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{1 013} P_0 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_7  }+ \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{1 033} P_0 v_3 S_3\, \tBmp_7  } \nonumber \\ &
		- \cancelto{0}{\frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{1 213} \bvec_2 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_8}- \cancelto{0}{\frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{1 233} \bvec_2 v_3 S_3\, \tBmp_8} - \cancelto{0}{\frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{1 133} \bvec_1 v_3 S_3\, \tBmp_8}- \cancelto{0}{\frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \epsilon^{1 113} \bvec_1 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_8}\nonumber \\ & 
        - \frac{\mN^3}{v \tcdot P} \cancelto{0}{(\bvec \tcdot S)} \underbrace{\epsilon^{1 023}}_{1} P_0 \bvec_2 v_3\, \tBmp_9 - \cancelto{0}{\frac{\mN^3}{v \tcdot P} (\bvec \tcdot S) \epsilon^{1 021} P_0 \bvec_2 v_1\, \tBmp_9} - \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \underbrace{\epsilon^{1 023}}_{-1} P_0 \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10} \nonumber \\ & - \cancelto{0}{\frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \epsilon^{1 021} P_0 \bvec_2 v_1 \tBmp_{10}}\\
  \Aboxed{ \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{1}]}_{\unsub} & =-|\vec{P}^1|\, \tAmp_2+ \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^1\, \tBmp_1 
		+ i \mN  P_0 \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P_0 \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10}- i \mN^2 \bvec^1\, \tAmp_3}
\end{align}
Evaluating the transverse component ($\mu=2$) isolates terms heavily dependent on $P_0$ and the orthogonal separation $b_1$:
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^2]}_{\unsub} & = 
		 - i \mN^2 \bvec^2\, \tAmp_3
		- i \mN \underbrace{\epsilon^{2 0 1 3}}_{1} P_0 \bvec_1 S_3\, \tAmp_{12} \nonumber \\ &
		~~
		+ \frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P} \underbrace{\epsilon^{2 0 1 3}}_{1} P_0 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_7  
		 \nonumber \\ &
		~~
		- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \underbrace{\epsilon^{2 0 1 3}}_{1} P_0 \bvec_1 v_3 \tBmp_{10}\\
  \Aboxed{\frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^2]}_{\unsub} & =- i \mN^2 \bvec^2\, \tAmp_3
		- i \mN  P_0 \bvec_1 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P}  P_0 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_7- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) P_0 \bvec_1 v_3 \tBmp_{10}}
\end{align}
Finally, the orthogonal transverse component ($\mu=3$) simplifies significantly due to the alignment of the spin vector:
\begin{align}
    \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^3]}_{\unsub} & = \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^3\, \tBmp_1 
		\nonumber \\ &
		~~
		- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) \underbrace{\epsilon^{3 0 2 1}}_{1} P_0 \bvec_2 v_1 \tBmp_{10}\\
  \Aboxed{\frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^3]}_{\unsub}& = \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^3\, \tBmp_1- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P_0 \bvec_2 v_1 \tBmp_{10}}
\end{align}
Consolidating these boxed results, we establish the full system of equations mapping the infinite-link continuous amplitudes to the projected correlator components:
\begin{align}
   \Aboxed{ \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{0}]}_{\unsub}& = P^0\, \tAmp_2  + i \mN  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P}  \bvec_2 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_8+ \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  |P_1| \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10}}\\
   \Aboxed{ \frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{1}]}_{\unsub} & =-|\vec{P}^1|\, \tAmp_2- i \mN^2 \bvec^1\, \tAmp_3+ \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^1\, \tBmp_1 
		+ i \mN  P_0 \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P_0 \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10}}\\
   \Aboxed{\frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^2]}_{\unsub} & =- i \mN^2 \bvec^2\, \tAmp_3
		- i \mN  P_0 \bvec_1 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P}  P_0 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_7- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) P_0 \bvec_1 v_3 \tBmp_{10}}\\
    \Aboxed{\frac{1}{2}\widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^3]}_{\unsub}& = \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^3\, \tBmp_1- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P_0 \bvec_2 v_1 \tBmp_{10}}
\end{align}
We structure this system as a column vector to streamline the upcoming algebraic inversions:
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{0}]}_{\unsub}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{1}]}_{\unsub}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{2}]}_{\unsub}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{3}]}_{\unsub}
    \end{pmatrix} &=2\begin{pmatrix}
       \rule{0pt}{16pt}  P^0\, \tAmp_2  + i \mN  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P}  \bvec_2 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_8+ \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  |P_1| \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} -|\vec{P}^1|\, \tAmp_2- i \mN^2 \bvec^1\, \tAmp_3+ \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^1\, \tBmp_1 
		+ i \mN  P_0 \bvec_2 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P_0 \bvec_2 v_3 \tBmp_{10}\\
         \rule{0pt}{16pt} - i \mN^2 \bvec^2\, \tAmp_3
		- i \mN  P_0 \bvec_1 S_3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P}  P_0 v_1 S_3\, \tBmp_7- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) P_0 \bvec_1 v_3 \tBmp_{10}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^3\, \tBmp_1- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P_0 \bvec_2 v_1 \tBmp_{10}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
To formally align with standard contravariant Lorentz notation, we raise the spatial indices using the Minkowski metric $g^{\mu\nu} = \text{diag}(1, -1, -1, -1)$. This introduces a relative minus sign for the spatial components (e.g., $P_1 = -P^1$, $b_2 = -b^2$):
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{0}]}_{\unsub}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{1}]}_{\unsub}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{2}]}_{\unsub}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \widetilde \Phi^{[\gamma^{3}]}_{\unsub}
    \end{pmatrix} &=2\begin{pmatrix}
       \rule{0pt}{16pt}  P^0\, \tAmp_2  - i \mN  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 S^3\, \tAmp_{12}- \frac{ i \mN^3}{v \tcdot P}  \bvec^2 v^1 S^3\, \tBmp_8- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 v^3 \tBmp_{10}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} -|\vec{P}^1|\, \tAmp_2- i \mN^2 \bvec^1\, \tAmp_3+ \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^1\, \tBmp_1 
		+ i \mN  P^0 \bvec^2 S^3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P^0 \bvec^2 v^3 \tBmp_{10}\\
         \rule{0pt}{16pt} - i \mN^2 \bvec^2\, \tAmp_3
		- i \mN  P^0 \bvec^1 S^3\, \tAmp_{12}+ \frac{\mN}{v \tcdot P}  P^0 v^1 S^3\, \tBmp_7- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S) P^0 \bvec^1 v^3 \tBmp_{10}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \frac{\mN^2}{(v \tcdot P)} v^3\, \tBmp_1- \frac{i \mN^3}{(v \tcdot P)^2} (v \tcdot S)  P^0 \bvec^2 v^1 \tBmp_{10}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
To connect these continuous amplitudes to the discretized finite-link observables simulated on the lattice, we apply the scaling dictionary bridging the infinite-link parametrization ($\tAmp_i$, $\tBmp_i$) to the finite-link parametrization ($\tilde{a}_i$, $\tilde{b}_i$):
\begin{align}
	\tAmp_i\left(\bvec^2,\bvec \tcdot P,\frac{v \tcdot \bvec}{v \tcdot P}, \frac{v^2}{(v \tcdot P)^2}, \eta v \tcdot P\right) & = \tilde a_i(\bvec^2,\bvec \tcdot P, \eta v \tcdot b, (\eta v)^2, \eta v \tcdot P)   \, , \nonumber \\
	 \tBmp_i\left(\bvec^2,\bvec \tcdot P,\frac{v \tcdot \bvec}{v \tcdot P}, \frac{v^2}{(v \tcdot P)^2}, \eta v \tcdot P\right) & = (\eta v \tcdot P)^n\ \tilde b_i(\bvec^2,\bvec \tcdot P, \eta v \tcdot b, (\eta v)^2, \eta v \tcdot P)   \, ,
	\label{eq-ab}
\end{align}
These invariant amplitudes are complex-valued functions, formally decomposed into their real and imaginary components:
\begin{align}
    \tilde{a}_i&=\tilde{a}^{\text{Re}}_i+i\tilde{a}^{\text{Im}}_i\\
    \tilde{b}_i&=\tilde{b}^{\text{Re}}_i+i\tilde{b}^{\text{Im}}_i
\end{align}
Furthermore, the lattice evaluation necessitates converting the continuous Minkowski gamma matrices ($\gamma_M$) into the strictly Euclidean conventions ($\gamma_E$) utilized by the lattice simulation software:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Correct Euclidean $\longleftrightarrow$ Minkowski $\gamma$-matrices notation:
    \begin{align}
        \Aboxed{\gamma^{1}_{E}=&+i\gamma^{1}_{M}}\\
        \Aboxed{\gamma^{2}_{E}=&-i\gamma^{2}_{M}}\\
        \Aboxed{\gamma^{3}_{E}=&+i\gamma^{3}_{M}}\\
        \Aboxed{\gamma^{4}_{E}=&\gamma^{4}_{M}}\\
        \boxed{\gamma^{5}_{E}}=&\gamma^{1}_{E}\gamma^{2}_{E}\gamma^{3}_{E}\gamma^{4}_{E}=\boxed{-\gamma^{5}_{M}}=-i\gamma^{4}_{M}\gamma^{1}_{M}\gamma^{2}_{M}\gamma^{3}_{M}
    \end{align}
\end{itemize}
Substituting the Euclidean gamma structures and mapping $P^0 \to E_N$, we rewrite the column vector strictly in terms of the lattice ratio observables $R_{\Gamma(i)}$ and the finite-link amplitudes:
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{\Gamma(1)}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{\Gamma(2)}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{\Gamma(4)}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{\Gamma(8)}
    \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{[+i\gamma^1]}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{[-i\gamma^2]}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{[+i\gamma^3]}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} R_{[\gamma^0]}
    \end{pmatrix} &=2\begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} -i\frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N}\, \tilde a_2+  \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^1\, \tilde a_3+ i\frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^1\, \tilde b_1 
		-  \mN  \bvec^2 \, \tilde a_{12}+  \mN^3  \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b_{10}\\
         \rule{0pt}{16pt} -  \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^2\, \tilde a_3
		-  \mN   \bvec^1  \tilde a_{12}-i \mN \eta  v^1  \tilde b_7+ \mN^3  \bvec^1 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b_{10}\\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} i\frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^3\, \tilde b_1-  \mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta^2 v^1 v^3\tilde b_{10}\\
         \rule{0pt}{16pt}   \tilde a_2  - i \mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a_{12}- \frac{ i \mN^3}{E_N}  \bvec^2 \eta v^1  \tilde b_8+ \frac{i \mN^3}{E_N}  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b_{10}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
To analytically separate the real and imaginary amplitude contributions, we split this matrix equation into two distinct sets by flipping the longitudinal separation component $b^1$. For a strictly positive longitudinal separation ($+b^1$), explicitly resolving the real and imaginary parts of the correlator ratios yields:
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[+b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[+b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[+b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[+b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[+b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[+b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[+b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[+b^1]}
    \end{pmatrix} &= 2\begin{pmatrix}
     \rule{0pt}{16pt}   \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^1\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_3-  \mN  \bvec^2 \, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}+  \mN^3  \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10} +\frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N}\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_2- \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^1\, \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_1\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -\frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N}\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_2+ \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^1\, \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_1 + \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^1\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_3-  \mN  \bvec^2 \, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}+  \mN^3  \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -  \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^2\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_3
		-  \mN   \bvec^1  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}+ \mN^3  \bvec^1 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}+\mN \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_7 \\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} - \mN \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_7 -  \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^2\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_3
		-  \mN   \bvec^1  \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}+ \mN^3  \bvec^1 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} -\mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta^2 v^1 v^3\tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}-\frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^3\, \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_1\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^3\, \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_1 -\mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta^2 v^1 v^3\tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_2 +  \mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}+ \frac{  \mN^3}{E_N}  \bvec^2 \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_8- \frac{ \mN^3}{E_N}  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} -  \mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}- \frac{  \mN^3}{E_N}  \bvec^2 \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_8+ \frac{ \mN^3}{E_N}  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}+\tilde a^{\text{Im}}_2
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
Evaluating the system for the negated separation ($-b^1$) introduces selective sign flips based on the parity constraints established in Section 3.5. By construction, the imaginary parts of the amplitudes are strictly odd functions of $b^1$ ($\tilde{A}^{\text{Im}}_i=\tilde{A}^{\text{(b-odd})}_i=\tilde{A}^{\text{($b^1$-odd})}_i$ and  $\tilde{B}^{\text{Im}}_i=\tilde{B}^{\text{(b-odd})}_i=\tilde{B}^{\text{($b^1$-odd})}_i$). Applying these parity reversals yields the conjugate matrix:
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[-b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[-b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[-b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[-b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[-b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[-b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[-b^1]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[-b^1]}
    \end{pmatrix} &= 2\begin{pmatrix}
     \rule{0pt}{16pt}   \textcolor{red}{-}\frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^1\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_3-  \mN  \bvec^2 \, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}+  \mN^3  \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10} \textcolor{red}{-}\frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N}\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_2\textcolor{red}{+} \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^1\, \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_1\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -\frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N}\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_2+ \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^1\, \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_1 + \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^1\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_3\textcolor{red}{+} \mN  \bvec^2 \, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}\textcolor{red}{-} \mN^3  \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  - \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^2\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_3
		\textcolor{red}{+}  \mN   \bvec^1  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}\textcolor{red}{-} \mN^3  \bvec^1 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}\textcolor{red}{-}\mN \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_7 \\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} - \mN \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_7 \textcolor{red}{+}  \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^2\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_3
		- \mN   \bvec^1  \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12} +\mN^3  \bvec^1 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}-\mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta^2 v^1 v^3\tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}\textcolor{red}{+}\frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^3\, \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_1\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^3\, \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_1 \textcolor{red}{+} \mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta^2 v^1 v^3\tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_2 \textcolor{red}{-}  \mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}\textcolor{red}{-} \frac{  \mN^3}{E_N}  \bvec^2 \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_8\textcolor{red}{+} \frac{ \mN^3}{E_N}  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} - \mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}- \frac{  \mN^3}{E_N}  \bvec^2 \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_8 + \frac{ \mN^3}{E_N}  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}\textcolor{red}{-}\tilde a^{\text{Im}}_2
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
By taking the symmetric average of the two matrices, $\frac{1}{2}\left(R^{[+b]} + R^{[-b]}\right)$, we mathematically project out the $b$-even combination, thereby naturally isolating the real parts of the requisite amplitudes:
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}
    \end{pmatrix} &= 2\begin{pmatrix}
     \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -  \mN  \bvec^2 \, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}+  \mN^3  \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10} \\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -\frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N}\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_2+ \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^1\, \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_1 + \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^1\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_3\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -  \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^2\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_3
		\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} - \mN \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_7
		-  \mN   \bvec^1  \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}+ \mN^3  \bvec^1 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} -\mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta^2 v^1 v^3\tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^3\, \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_1 \\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_2\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} -  \mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}- \frac{  \mN^3}{E_N}  \bvec^2 \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_8+ \frac{ \mN^3}{E_N}  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
Conversely, taking the antisymmetric half-difference of the two matrices, $\frac{1}{2}\left(R^{[+b]} - R^{[-b]}\right)$, projects out the $b$-odd combination, strictly isolating the imaginary parts:
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(2)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} \\
        \rule{0pt}{16pt} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}
    \end{pmatrix} &= 2\begin{pmatrix}
     \rule{0pt}{16pt}   \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^1\, \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_3+\frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N}\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_2- \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^1\, \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_1\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -  \mN  \bvec^2 \, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}+  \mN^3  \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  
		-  \mN   \bvec^1  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}+ \mN^3  \bvec^1 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Re}}_{10}+\mN \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_7 \\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -  \frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \bvec^2\, \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_3\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} -\frac{\mN^2}{E_N} \eta v^3\, \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_1\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}  -\mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta^2 v^1 v^3\tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt}    \mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}+ \frac{  \mN^3}{E_N}  \bvec^2 \eta v^1  \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_8- \frac{ \mN^3}{E_N}  |\vec{P}^1| \bvec^2 \eta^2 (v^3)^2 \tilde b^{\text{Im}}_{10}\\
      \rule{0pt}{16pt} \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_2
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{align}
To analytically reconstruct the Sivers shift, we must solve these projected systems for the subset of amplitudes driving the Sivers effect: specifically, $\tilde a_2$, $\tilde b_1$, $\tilde a_{12}$, and $\tilde b_8$. Reading directly from the $b$-even system, we isolate the real components:
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tilde a^{\text{Re}}_2 = & \frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} }\\
    \Aboxed{\tilde b^{\text{Re}}_1 = & \frac{E_{N}}{2\mN^2 \eta v^3} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}}
\end{align}
Substituting the appropriate matrix rows, we dynamically decouple $\tilde a_{12}$ and $\tilde b_8$:
\begin{align}
   \Aboxed{\tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}=&\frac{-1}{2\mN  \bvec^2}\left[\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}+\frac{v^3}{v^1}\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}\right]}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tilde b^{\text{Re}}_8=&\frac{-E_N}{ 2\mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta v^1 }\left[\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}+\frac{|\vec{P}^1|v^3}{E_N v^1}\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}+ 2\mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Re}}_{12}\right]}
\end{align}
Applying identical algebraic decoupling to the $b$-odd system, we isolate the corresponding imaginary components:
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tilde a^{\text{Im}}_2 = & \frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} }\\
    \Aboxed{\tilde b^{\text{Im}}_1 = & \frac{-E_{N}}{2\mN^2 \eta v^3} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}}
\end{align}
and the remaining transverse amplitudes:
\begin{align}
   \Aboxed{\tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}=&\frac{-1}{2\mN  \bvec^2}\left[\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}+\frac{v^3}{v^1}\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}\right]}
\end{align}
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tilde b^{\text{Im}}_8=&\frac{E_N}{ 2\mN^3 \bvec^2 \eta v^1 }\left[\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}-\frac{|\vec{P}^1|v^3}{E_N v^1}\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}- 2\mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tilde a^{\text{Im}}_{12}\right]}
\end{align}
Finally, we convert these finite-link evaluations ($\tilde a_i, \tilde b_i$) back into the standard continuum-invariant amplitudes ($\tAmp_i, \tBmp_i$) using the proportionality dictionary:
\begin{align}
	\tAmp_i & = \tilde a_i \\
	 \tBmp_i & = (\eta v \tcdot P)^n\ \tilde b_i
\end{align}
Recalling that $v \tcdot P = v_1 P^1 = v^1 |P^1|$ due to our chosen frame, the conversion factors uniquely scale each amplitude structure. Applying this mapping to the real components yields the final accessible amplitudes on the lattice:
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tAmp^{\text{Re}}_2 = & \frac{1}{2}\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]} }\\
    \Aboxed{\tBmp^{\text{Re}}_1 = & \frac{E_{N}|P^1|v^1}{2\mN^2  v^3} \text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}}\\
    \Aboxed{\tAmp^{\text{Re}}_{12}=&\frac{-1}{2\mN  \bvec^2}\left[\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}+\frac{v^3}{v^1}\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}\right]}\\
    \Aboxed{\tBmp^{\text{Re}}_8=&\frac{-E_N |P^1|}{ 2\mN^3 \bvec^2 }\left[\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}+\frac{|\vec{P}^1|v^3}{E_N v^1}\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-even}]}+ 2\mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tAmp^{\text{Re}}_{12}\right]}
\end{align}
Applying the same scaling factors to the imaginary counterparts provides the complementary T-odd contributions:
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tAmp^{\text{Im}}_2 = & \frac{1}{2}\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]} }\\
    \Aboxed{\tBmp^{\text{Im}}_1 = & \frac{-E_{N}|P^1|v^1}{2\mN^2 \eta v^3} \text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}}\\
    \Aboxed{\tAmp^{\text{Im}}_{12}=&\frac{-1}{2\mN  \bvec^2}\left[\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(1)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}+\frac{v^3}{v^1}\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}\right]}\\
     \Aboxed{\tBmp^{\text{Im}}_8=&\frac{E_N|P^1|}{ 2\mN^3 \bvec^2  }\left[\text{Re}\{ R_{\Gamma(8)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}-\frac{|\vec{P}^1|v^3}{E_N v^1}\text{Im}\{ R_{\Gamma(4)}\}^{[b^{1}\text{-odd}]}- 2\mN  \frac{|\vec{P}^1|}{E_N} \bvec^2  \tAmp^{\text{Im}}_{12}\right]}
\end{align}
With the fundamental invariant amplitudes explicitly extracted, the final step to reconstruct the effective amplitudes governing the unpolarized and Sivers TMDs ($\tAmp_{2B}$ and $\tAmp_{12B}$ respectively) is to combine them according to the kinematic constraint of the Collins-Soper parameter:
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{\tAmp_{2B}  & \ \equiv\  \tAmp_2 + R(\zetahat^2) \tBmp_1}\\
    \Aboxed{\tAmp_{12B}  & \ \equiv\  \tAmp_{12} - R(\zetahat^2) \tBmp_8}
\end{align}
where the geometric regularization factor characterizing the approach to the light cone is strictly defined by:
\begin{align}
    \Aboxed{R(\zetahat^2) \equiv 1- \sqrt{1 + \hat \zeta^{-2}}}
\end{align}
