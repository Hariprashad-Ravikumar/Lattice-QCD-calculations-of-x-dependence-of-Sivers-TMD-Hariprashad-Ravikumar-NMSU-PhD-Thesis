We compute the dependence of the Sivers function, a transverse momentum dependent parton distribution (TMD), on the quark longitudinal momentum fraction $x$ in lattice QCD with dynamical 2+1-flavor clover-improved Wilson fermions at a lattice spacing of $a\approx 0.114$~fm. The TMDs are defined through non-local quark bilinears with staple-shaped, process-dependent Wilson lines, and the observable is the generalized Sivers shift, a ratio of Lorentz-invariant amplitudes. Earlier lattice calculations of this shift were restricted to $b\cdot P=0$ and therefore gave only its integral over $x$, the first Mellin moment. We scan the full accessible range of $b\cdot P$, the variable Fourier conjugate to $x$. At nonzero $b\cdot P$, the kinematic constraint that fixes the Collins-Soper parameter $\hat\zeta$ requires staples along off-axis directions of the Euclidean lattice. We construct them with a symmetry improvement, averaging the correlators over degenerate lattice paths, followed by interpolation to the off-axis directions. A two-dimensional Gaussian fitted to the unpolarized amplitude is not integrable in $b\cdot P$, so its Fourier transform to $x$ does not exist. Symbolic regression with PySR points instead to a power-law dependence on $b^2$, and we fit all amplitudes simultaneously with their full jackknife covariance at four nucleon momenta. After extrapolation to the light-cone limit $\hat\zeta\to\infty$, the Sivers shift $\langle k_y\rangle_{TU}$ at a transverse quark separation of $0.34$~fm peaks at about $0.21$~GeV at small $x$ and falls to near zero by $x\approx 0.5$, while $x\langle k_y\rangle_{TU}$ peaks at about $0.02$~GeV near $x\approx 0.15$. The shift factorizes into a function of $x$ times a function of $|b_T|$ to within 5.7 to 7.6 percent in the relative $L_2$ norm.
