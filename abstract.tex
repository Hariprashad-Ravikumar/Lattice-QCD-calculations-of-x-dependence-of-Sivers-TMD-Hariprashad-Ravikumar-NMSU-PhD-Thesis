\hspace{\parindent}
We compute the dependence of the Sivers shift on the quark longitudinal momentum fraction $x$ in lattice QCD with 2+1 flavors of clover-improved Wilson fermions at a lattice spacing of \mbox{$a\approx 0.114$~fm}. The transverse momentum dependent parton distributions (TMDs) are defined through quark bilinears with staple-shaped Wilson lines. The generalized Sivers shift $\langle k_y\rangle_{TU}=m_N\,\tilde f_{1T}^{\perp(1)}/\tilde f_1^{(0)}$, the ratio of the Sivers TMD to the unpolarized TMD, is obtained from a ratio of Lorentz-invariant amplitudes. To extract the $x$-dependence, we scan the full accessible range of $b\cdot P$, the variable Fourier conjugate to $x$. This requires staples along off-axis lattice directions, which we obtain by interpolation. Using the PySR symbolic regression and statistical modeling, we systematically explored several ans\"atze that satisfy physical constraints, and we describe the amplitudes at four nucleon momenta with a global fit in $b\cdot P$, $b^2$, and the staple length $\eta$. After extrapolation to the light-cone limit $\hat\zeta\to\infty$, the $x$-weighted Sivers shift $x\langle k_y\rangle_{TU}$ at $|b_T|=0.34$~fm is about $0.006$--$0.011$~GeV for $0.3\le x\le 0.4$. For $0.34\le|b_T|\le 0.94$~fm, the Sivers shift is close to, but not exactly, a product of a function of $x$ and a function of $|b_T|$, with a relative distance of 6--8\% from the best factorized form.
